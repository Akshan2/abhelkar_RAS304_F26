    Driver PIC 1 (Servo + Display)

    This page covers the components for Driver PIC 1: the joint-1 servo and the diagnostic display. Driver 1 receives target positions from the Main Processor PIC, drives the joint, shows leg status on the display, and reports status back.

    Selection criteria used for both components

    • Runs on 5V.
    • Has a manufacturer or available datasheet.
    • Uses few pins and easy to integrate.

    Hip Joint Servo (Rotary Actuator)

    1. Hitec HS-485HB standard analog servo

      • $28.99/each
      • HS-485HB product page at Amzaon
      • HS-485HB manufacturer datasheet
      Pros Cons
      Position control is built in: the internal potentiometer holds the commanded angle, so the PIC only outputs one 50 Hz PWM signal and no driver IC is needed. Stall current is 1.0 A at 4.8 V and 1.2 A at 6 V per the datasheet, so the regulator and wiring must be sized for it, and the servo cannot be powered from the PIC's 5V pin.
      Stall torque of 4.8 kgf·cm at 4.8 V and 6.0 kgf·cm at 6 V in a 45 g package gives the highest torque per gram of the three options. Karbonite (plastic) gears can strip under shock loads such as a leg strike, and metal-gear servos cost more.
      It runs from the same 4.8–6 V range as the logic, and the manufacturer datasheet lists stall current, idle current, speed, and dimensions, which makes the power budget straightforward. The servo gives no position output, so the Nano cannot confirm that the joint reached the angle. The status sent to the Main Nano is the commanded value, not a measured one.

    2. NEMA 17 bipolar stepper (17HS15-1704S) with DRV8825 stepper driver carrier

      • Motor: $9.98
      • 17HS4023S Stepper motor product page at Amazon
      • 17HS15-1704S full datasheet

      • Driver: $15.95
      • DRV8825 driver product page at Pololu
      • DRV8825 datasheet (Texas Instruments)
      Pros Cons
      The motor's rated current of about 0.7 A per phase is well under the DRV8825 carrier's roughly 1.5 A limit without a heat sink, so no extra cooling is needed. Holding torque is only about 14 N·cm (roughly 1.4 kgf·cm), about a third of the HS-485HB's 4.8 kgf·cm at 4.8 V, so a gearbox would likely be needed for a hip joint, adding cost, backlash, and mass.
      The DRV8825 provides up to 1/32 microstepping from a 1.8° step motor, and the PIC only has to output STEP and DIR pulses with no PWM timing constraints. The DRV8825 needs an 8.2–45 V motor supply (the motor is rated for 12 V), so it cannot run from the 5–6 V logic rail and adds a second power rail, regulator, and power budget line.
      The shaft holds position while the coils are energized with no gear train to strip, and the DRV8825 adds over current protection, thermal shutdown, and an adjustable current limit. It is open loop with no position feedback, so lost steps go undetected and the joint needs a homing switch at power up, and the coils draw current continuously while holding.

    Choice: Option 1: Hitec HS-485HB standard servo

    Reason: The HS-485HB is the best match for the Driver PIC 1 architecture. Its internal closed loop position control means the PIC only needs one pin, and it runs from a 4.8–6 V supply. The stepper option (Option 2) is the most inexpensive motor, but the DRV8825 needs at least 8.2 V, which forces a second different voltage power rail and regulator, and the 280 g motor is about six times heavier than the servo for similar torque.

    Diagnostic Display (leg status)

    1. 2.42 inches 128x64 IIC I2C OLED Display Module (Amazon)

      • Price: $15.59
      • 2.42 Inches I2C OLED Display Module on Amazon
      • Oled Display Datasheet
      Pros Cons
      Large 2.42-inch, 128×64 display provides clear text and enough space for angles, status, and fault messages. The display controller requires more code to run.
      Uses only four I²C connections: VCC, GND, SDA, and SCL. It is larger and more expensive than common 0.96-inch OLED modules.
      Supports 3–5 V power and provides bright, high contrast graphics without a backlight. Static images displayed for long periods can cause pixel dimming or burn in.

    2. Adafruit Standard 16×2 character LCD

      • Price: $9.95
      • Adafruit Standard LCD 16×2 (ADA181) product page
      • Adafruit Standard LCD 16×2 Display Code Datasheet
      • Adafruit Standard LCD 16×2 Display Wiring Datasheet
      Pros Cons
      Adafruit provides a datasheet with pin locations and the HD44780 command set datasheet. It is text-only (16 characters × 2 rows), so it cannot show graphics or a compact status layout.
      The single-row 0.1" header suits breadboards and through-hole boards. It needs multiple digital lines in 4-bit mode (versus two for an I2C OLED). Adding the I2C backpack would reduce the pins but add cost and put it on the I2C bus.
      The Liquid Crystal is easy to program with well documented user guides, also it avoids the OLED frame buffer issue. A contrast potentiometer and backlight resistor are needed, and the backlight draws current continuously.
      The blue LED backlight can be dimmed with a resistor or PWM and uses much less power than backlights. The module is physically larger than the OLED.

    Choice: Option 1: 2.42 inches 128x64 IIC I2C OLED Display Module

    Reason: The OLED can show multiple lines of leg status (commanded angle, measured status, fault flags) and simple graphics, which the 16×2 LCD cannot. It needs only two signal pins, whereas the LCD needs six or more

    Pin Table (Potential Pin Connection Plan, Driver PIC 1)

    PIC pin Function Connects to
    RC2 7 Servo PWM output
    RB2 9 I2C SDA
    RB1 10 I2C SCL
    RC4 11 Spare GPIO
    RC5 12 Spare GPIO
    RF0 25 UART TX
    RF1 26 UART RX
    RF4 34 Debug LED output
    RF7 37 Debug push-button input
    VTG 51 Board power input

    Peripheral Connector Wiring

    Connector Pin Connection
    J2 Servo 1 Servo PWM signal from RC2 through R1
    J2 Servo 2 +5V_SERVO from JP1
    J2 Servo 3 Earth
    J3 OLED 1 Earth
    J3 OLED 2 Main +5 V
    J3 OLED 3 I2C SCL from RB1
    J3 OLED 4 I2C SDA from RB2
    J5 Expansion 1 Earth
    J5 Expansion 2 RC5
    J5 Expansion 3 RC4
    J5 Expansion 4 Main +5 V

    The HS-485HB servo is powered by a separate LM7805 regulator. Jumper-1 allows the servo supply to be disconnected during testing. The OLED and PIC development board use the main regulated +5 V supply. All other pins have a common Earth connection.
